Route 108: the Abandoned Ship is a ship now, not a green slab

What you saw: the wreck sticking out of the sea was a low green platform with a symmetrical beige
structure on top - it read as a building in the water, not a wrecked ship.

Why: like the boat, it came out of the drawing as a height field. tools/buildings/hf.py turned every
art row of the wreck's drawing into depth, so the hulk stood up 26 px as terraces: the hull's side
view (its portholes, its algae waterline) became a slab of ground, and the deck's plan became blocks.

Now: lib/AbandonedWreck.lua models the drawing as the ship it is - one hull with a pointed bow, a
raised foredeck and poop deck, a bulwark round the deck, a broken rail by the bow, an open cargo hold
with its coaming and its flooded floor, and a deckhouse whose aft third has fallen in. Every face is
painted with a pixel of the same composed drawing the old model used (the hull's strake rows for the
topsides, the plan for the decks and the hold), so the colours and the portholes are the art's own;
heights are lifted 1.4x because a drawing made at the map's 3/4 angle foreshortens height.

The model also lays the shallow water it stands in: a claimed cell loses its own ground plane, and
the old height field happened to cover the whole box. The water sits just under the waterline so an
unclaimed neighbour's own plane still wins.

Machinery: BuildingVoxels gets an AUTHORED table - a hand-designed geometry table swapped in for one
generated model by name; that model's anchor, tiles and box stay generated (they decide where it
applies and how its drawing is composed). lib/AbandonedWreck.lua is generated by the new
tools/buildings/wreck.py, which also renders previews of the mesh offline.
